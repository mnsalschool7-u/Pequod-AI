The border problem

Global trade still breaks at the border.

Every imported product has to be classified before it can move through customs. That classification helps determine what the product is, which regulations apply, and what duties or tariffs may be charged.

But products do not always fit neatly into one category. A classification decision can affect landed cost, trigger additional requirements, or contribute to a shipment being delayed while the importer tries to understand what went wrong.

When a shipment gets held, teams can end up jumping between customs notices, tariff schedules, classification databases, broker emails, invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and government guidance just to answer one question:

Why is my shipment not moving? Every additional day can mean demurrage, detention, storage fees, inventory delays, and capital trapped in goods the business cannot access.

Founder story

Built from firsthand experience moving goods across borders.

I got involved in TradeTech through TerraLuxCraftworks, my Himalayan pink salt company. I import products including Himalayan pink salt lamps, candles, foot detoxers, and urns directly from suppliers in Pakistan.

When I started TerraLux, I assumed the hardest part would be finding customers or navigating Amazon. It was not. One of the hardest moments came when a six figure cargo shipment became unexpectedly delayed at the Port of Colombo in Sri Lanka.

Every additional day meant more demurrage and detention exposure, delayed inventory, and capital trapped inside goods I could not access. Working backward through the shipment forced me to understand how customs duties, classification, and tariff treatment could materially change the economics of a transaction.

Why is it still so difficult for businesses to understand what a shipment will cost, what customs expects, and what to do when something goes wrong?

That question became Pequod.

Why now

Customs complexity is becoming more consequential.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection processes tens of millions of import entries every year. At the same time, tariff regimes, trade policies, product classifications, and compliance requirements continue to change.

Customs and classification are among the most consequential bottlenecks in cross border trade. The cost of getting classification, tariff treatment, or customs documentation wrong can extend far beyond the duty itself.

Higher landed cost Delayed release Storage charges Demurrage and detention Trapped inventory
Technology

Document intelligence built for trade workflows.

Pequod’s backend uses LlamaIndex and LlamaParse to ingest and structure complex, unstructured trade documents. After evaluating Pequod’s use case, LlamaIndex provided startup credits that helped subsidize development of the document intelligence infrastructure.

Pequod is designed as a decision support and workflow layer for importers, exporters, and customs professionals, not as a replacement for licensed customs brokers.
